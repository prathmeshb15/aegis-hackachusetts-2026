    How AEGIS works

    AEGIS sits between the robot's planner and its motors. It doesn't replace the autonomy stack. Every 100 ms it asks one question: if I send this command, can the robot still stop safely, whatever the people nearby do next?

    1 · PerceiveTracks people, forklifts and pallets within sensor range. Shelves block line of sight, so hidden pedestrians appear late.
    2 · ForecastPredicts each object 2 s ahead. The uncertainty disk grows with lookahead time, sensor noise and how confident the velocity estimate is.
    3 · VerifySimulates candidate commands (proceed, slow, stop, evade, retreat) with the robot's real braking limit, then keeps the first one that preserves clearance.
    4 · Act or replanSends the verified command. If the route is blocked it replans with A*. If nothing is safe, it takes the best brake/evade manoeuvre and logs that openly.
    Where this approach could apply
    • Warehouse and logistics robots sharing aisles with staff and forklifts
    • Hospital and hotel delivery robots in busy corridors
    • Factory tuggers and AGVs at blind intersections
    • A transparent, auditable layer on top of existing navigation stacks, explaining why the robot slowed down
    What it is and isn't
    • It is a rule-based, physics-checked safety filter that you can inspect frame by frame.
    • It is not machine learning, not certified safety software, and not tested on a physical robot.
    • Pedestrians here are scripted and never yield. Real deployment would need real sensors, hardware validation and safety certification.
